What is the 50/30/20 Rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is the simplest budgeting rule in the US — and the easiest to stick to. It splits your take-home pay into three buckets so you never have to wonder where your money should go.

50%

Needs

Rent or mortgage, groceries, utilities, car payment, insurance, gas, minimum debt payments. The bills that keep your life running.

30%

Wants

Dining out, streaming subscriptions, travel, hobbies, shopping. The extras that make life fun — and are the easiest to trim.

20%

Savings & Debt

Emergency fund, retirement, extra debt payments beyond the minimum, or saving for a home or car.

50% Needs30% Wants20% Savings

Example: if you earn $3,000 per month after taxes, put $1,500 toward needs (rent, groceries, utilities), $900 toward wants (dining out, subscriptions, travel), and $600 toward savings and debt.

To set it up in the tracker above, add three categories — Needs, Wants and Savings — and set the planned amounts to 50%, 30% and 20% of your paycheck. Then log every expense under the right category. If a bucket goes over, you'll see it instantly and can adjust before the month ends.

What is Zero-Based Budgeting?

Zero-based budgeting means every dollar gets a job. You keep assigning money to categories until Income − Expenses − Savings = $0.

"Zero" does not mean you spend everything — savings is a category too. It simply means no dollar is left floating without a purpose. If you get a $3,500 paycheck and only plan $3,000 of it, that extra $500 is the most likely money to disappear on impulse purchases.

Zero-based budgeting fixes that. You decide in advance where every dollar goes, so the end of the month never surprises you. It works especially well with paycheck budgeting, because you plan around the exact amount that landed in your account.

This tracker is built for zero-based budgeting. When your remaining balance hits zero, your plan is complete and you know exactly where all of your money is going.

It is a simple guideline that splits your after-tax income into 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and debt. For example, on a $3,000 paycheck that means $1,500 for needs, $900 for wants and $600 for savings.
