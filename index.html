What is the 50/30/20 Rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple budgeting guideline that splits your after-tax income into three buckets: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt repayment. It is easy to remember and works in any currency, which makes it a great starting point for a first monthly budget.

Example: if your monthly income is 3,000 in your currency, you would put 1,500 toward needs (rent, groceries, utilities, transport, insurance), 900 toward wants (dining out, subscriptions, travel, hobbies) and 600 toward savings or extra debt payments.

In the tracker above, create three categories — Needs, Wants and Savings — and set the limits to 50%, 30% and 20% of your income. Then log your expenses under the right category. If a category goes over its limit, you will see it immediately and can adjust your spending before the month ends.

What is Zero-Based Budgeting?

Zero-based budgeting means every unit of your income is assigned to a category until Income − Expenses − Savings = 0. Zero does not mean you spend everything — savings is a category too. It simply means no money is left unassigned, so every unit has a job before you spend it.

For example, if you earn 2,000 in your currency, you might assign 700 to rent, 300 to groceries, 200 to transport, 150 to utilities, 250 to savings and 400 to personal spending. Add them up and you get exactly 2,000 — a zero-based budget.

This tracker is built for zero-based budgeting. When your remaining balance reaches zero, your plan is complete and you know exactly where every unit of your income is going.
